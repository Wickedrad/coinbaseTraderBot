# What the literature says about calling the next 15 minutes in BTC and ETH (Scite search, 2026-11-19 5:45 PM EST / 22:45 UTC)

Research only. Written before any sweep is scored. Method: Scite `search_literature` (five searches), `check_claim` on eight claims, `verify_papers` on every paper cited (14 of 14 verified, no retraction or expression of concern, none in a predatory journal), decisions recorded with `report_citations` (13 cited, 3 excluded). Most hits were read at abstract and Smart Citation level, not full text (several are paywalled, `contentDenied`), so each line says what was actually seen.

## What is supported, and what we do with it

| # | Finding | Source (verified) | What was seen | Used as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At 15-minute horizons directional reversal is far stronger in crypto than in US equities: 90% of 183 Binance pairs show significant reversal under one strictly out-of-sample protocol. "Simply betting against the previous candle captures most of the effect." Reversal concentrates after moves driven by aggressive taker flow and grows with flow intensity; order-book depth consumed conditions nothing. Gross edge peaks near 1.3 bp per trade against a 5 bp round trip: detectable, too small to clear spot costs. Survives a permutation null and a frozen six-month holdout (AUC gap +0.011, 95% CI +0.008 to +0.014 under the most conservative accounting). | Kitron and Wengrowicz 2026, arXiv 2608.21888 (10.48550/arxiv.2608.21888) | abstract | The side rule (fade) and the flow filter (taker-buy imbalance of the move). |
| 2 | Crypto trading and price variation burst at every 1-, 5- and 15-minute mark. Returns and order flow at quarter-hour openings show serial dependence; the autocorrelation at those marks is sharply negative (reversal). Quarter-hour opening returns are predictable out of sample; opening order imbalance predicts returns over 4 to 12 hours, with much weaker effects at finer frequencies. | Kim and Hansen 2026, arXiv 2607.09426 (10.48550/arxiv.2607.09426) | abstract and Smart Citation statements | The clock-phase filter (minute of the quarter hour). Order imbalance is NOT used as a 15-minute predictor: the paper puts its predictive content at 4 to 12 hours. |

## What the literature does not give us

- A 15-minute call that clears costs. The best-documented effect (item 1) is about 52 to 53% direction with a gross edge of about 1.3 bp. Our own H2 timing on 2026 unseen days (54.0 to 54.5%) is already above that, so a sweep should not be expected to find 58%. Whether a call pays after Kalshi fees is phase 2.
- Support for "high-volume moves reverse and low-volume moves continue" at this horizon. The one match `check_claim` returned was a stock-momentum paper over years (Lee and Swaminathan 2000, excluded). The volume filter stays in the grid as a question, not as a finding.
- Anything on Kalshi contract prices. All papers measure the coin or its perpetual.

## Excluded

10.21203/rs.3.rs-11272412/v1 (a preprint on LLM-written strategies and exposure-matched nulls; exists, not read, not relied on); 10.1111/0022-1082.00280 (volume and momentum over years in stocks; wrong horizon); 10.1007/978-3-030-37110-4_10 (a chapter quoting a 52% LSTM accuracy second-hand).
